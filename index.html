Withdrawal order is a tax rate you pick

Retirement math puts its energy into the savings rate and the draw rate, and then spends the accounts in whatever order the login page opens. The draw order carries a rate of its own. The same $40,000 of spending can arrive with a $2,762 tax bill attached or with none, and the only thing that changed is which account got tapped first. The withdrawal-order side of the problem gets less airtime than sequence risk at FireNomics, and it is the lever that needs no market timing at all.

Take a $1,000,000 retirement split the ordinary way: $400,000 in a taxable brokerage where half the position is gain (basis $200,000) and $600,000 in a pre-tax IRA. Spending need: $40,000 a year.

IRA first: the $40,000 withdrawal is ordinary income. The standard deduction absorbs $15,000 and leaves $25,000 taxable. The 10 percent bracket covers the first $11,925 and 12 percent covers the rest, so $1,193 plus $1,569 comes to $2,762, a 6.9 percent effective rate on the draw. Spendable cash lands at $37,238, and the gap gets closed by selling taxable shares the same year.

Taxable first: sell $40,000 of stock with a $20,000 embedded gain. Only the gain is income, and a $20,000 long-term gain under the roughly $48,000 single-filer threshold sits in the 0 percent bracket. The bill is $0 and the full $40,000 arrives. The IRA's $600,000 stays put and keeps compounding for the years that will actually be taxed.

Ten years of drawing the IRA first pays $27,620 in tax that the other order never owes. The 0 percent bracket is an annual space that expires unused if the taxable account is saved for later, and waiting until required minimum distributions stack on top just pushes both incomes into the same years. The withdrawal order math is an ordering rule: fill the 0 percent space first, touch ordinary income last.

Tally: $400k taxable (basis $200k) + $600k pre-tax IRA, $40k/yr need. IRA-first: $25k taxable income = $1,193 at 10% + $1,569 at 12% = $2,762 tax = 6.9% of the draw, net $37,238. Taxable-first: $20k LTCG in the 0% bracket, $0 tax, net $40,000. Ten years: $27,620 of tax on the wrong order.
